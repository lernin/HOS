# Bathmonym — Ten-Level Vocabulary Model

# Bathmonym

## The ten-level Procedia teaching sequence — revised GSE/CEFR model (v0.2.1)

## Executive definition

Bathmonym is the ten-level curricular placement assigned to an admitted vocabulary sense in Procedia’s selected instructional core. The name combines Greek βαθμός (bathmós: step, stair, degree, or rank) with \-onym (name). A Bathmonym answers one precise question: “At which instructional level should this vocabulary meaning be taught?”  
Naming the construct matters. Once Bathmonym has a stable name, we can discuss its boundaries, evidence, levels, errors, and revisions without confusing it with CEFR, frequency, taxonomy, or Commonality.  
Bathmonym is not a frequency score, not a school grade, and not a WordNet relation. Hypernymy remains reserved for “X is a kind of Y.” Commonality remains a separate open-world estimate of how common or urgent an encountered sense is.

## The central revision

The earlier draft treated CEFR, NGSL, NAWL, and AWL as if they were consecutive blocks on one ladder. That architecture was wrong. CEFR is a proficiency framework; NGSL is a general-frequency list; NAWL is an academic-frequency extension designed to work with NGSL; and AWL is an older academic list. They provide different kinds of evidence and must be integrated, not stacked.  
Bathmonym now uses the ten published GSE/CEFR bands as its structural spine. This gives Procedia exactly ten pedagogically meaningful steps while keeping the familiar CEFR interpretation.

## The ten levels

* L1 — Pre-A1 — GSE 10–21 — earliest functional foundation.  
* L2 — A1 — GSE 22–29 — basic beginner vocabulary.  
* L3 — A2 — GSE 30–35 — elementary vocabulary, lower band.  
* L4 — A2+ — GSE 36–42 — elementary vocabulary, upper band.  
* L5 — B1 — GSE 43–50 — independent-user vocabulary, lower band.  
* L6 — B1+ — GSE 51–58 — independent-user vocabulary, upper band.  
* L7 — B2 — GSE 59–66 — upper-independent vocabulary, lower band.  
* L8 — B2+ — GSE 67–75 — upper-independent vocabulary, upper band.  
* L9 — C1 — GSE 76–84 — advanced vocabulary.  
* L10 — C2 — GSE 85–90 — highly advanced vocabulary.

## What GSE is

Pearson’s Global Scale of English (GSE) is a granular proficiency scale running operationally from 10 to 90\. It does not replace CEFR; it divides the broad CEFR bands into smaller measurable intervals. That is why it is useful here: A2, B1, and B2 can each become a lower and a “plus” Bathmonym level without inventing arbitrary boundaries.  
CEFR itself is primarily a can-do proficiency framework, not a single official vocabulary list. Sense-level lexical evidence is supplied more directly by resources such as the English Vocabulary Profile and Pearson’s GSE vocabulary work.

## Pearson’s young-learner vocabulary research

Pearson’s Developing GSE Vocabulary for Young Learners was designed for learners approximately ages 6–11 and contains about 3,000 lexical units, where a lexical unit is a word meaning rather than merely a spelling. Its method is highly relevant to Bathmonym’s early levels:

* build frequency evidence from children’s first-language corpora and learning materials;  
* separate and annotate meanings and topics;  
* ask primary teachers to rate usefulness;  
* use statistical modeling to place the vocabulary on the proficiency scale.

Procedia should use this as a validation model and, if licensing and data access permit, as direct placement evidence. We should not assume that all 3,000 units can simply be copied into the database.

## Approximate grade and age alignment

There is a positive relationship between vocabulary proficiency, age, and school grade, but there is no universal conversion such as “A2 equals Grade 5.” The result changes with country, hours of English instruction, bilingual exposure, reading volume, curriculum, and whether the learner is being measured in a first or additional language.  
For planning only, the current teaching-stage hypothesis is:

* L1–L2: early-primary or first-exposure EFL foundation.  
* L3–L4: developing elementary EFL.  
* L5–L6: upper-elementary to lower-secondary EFL.  
* L7–L8: secondary independent-user range.  
* L9–L10: advanced secondary, university-preparation, or academic range.

These are provisional teaching-stage labels, not grade equivalences. Each should be validated against child age-of-acquisition data, school-text frequency, learner performance, and the curriculum actually used with Procedia students.  
For the Korean context, the 2022 Revised National Curriculum Basic Vocabulary List is a particularly important local validation source. It contains 3,000 word families and provides a school-system reference point. Because it counts word families rather than WordNet senses, it must be normalized and sense-matched before it can influence Bathmonym.

## Evidence sources and their proper roles

* CEFR: the broad proficiency anchor. It tells us the kind of communicative competence associated with A1–C2; it is not itself one additive vocabulary block.  
* English Vocabulary Profile: sense- and phrase-level CEFR evidence. This is closer to Bathmonym’s unit of placement than a headword list.  
* GSE: the ten-band calibration spine. It refines CEFR into Pre-A1, A1, A2, A2+, B1, B1+, B2, B2+, C1, and C2.  
* Pearson Young Learner Vocabulary: child-specific evidence for early levels, especially ages 6–11 and approximately 3,000 lexical meanings.  
* Fry: older high-frequency and recognition evidence for the early foundation. Useful as one signal, not as the definition of L1–L3.  
* NGSL: 2,809 modern general-service headwords and a strong frequency/coverage signal. NGSL words do not come “after CEFR”; they can occur across many CEFR levels.  
* NAWL: 957 academic headwords designed to work after the NGSL coverage base. It is an academic extension, but its words must still be placed by actual difficulty and usefulness rather than forced above every general word.  
* AWL: 570 legacy academic headwords. It remains valuable for provenance and gap auditing, but it should not define a separate Bathmonym level.  
* Korean 2022 curriculum vocabulary: local grade-stage validation for Korean schools, after normalization from word families to Procedia senses.  
* Age of acquisition and concreteness: child-learnability signals. Existing research datasets cover roughly 30,000 English content words for age of acquisition and more than 37,000 words for concreteness.  
* Commonality L1–L5: a separate open-world priority coordinate. It can help break ties or delay an unanchored sense, but it is not a Bathmonym level.  
* WordNet taxonomy: conceptual structure. Parent-before-child is only advisory. Children can learn dog before organism; the real sequencing question is whether a concept or definition is needed to understand another item.

## AWL and NAWL normalization decision

The raw AWL should not be deleted. In the present inventory, 486 of its 570 headwords are exact matches in NGSL or NAWL. The remaining 84 exact residuals are a pre-normalization audit set and include spelling and word-family variants. Operationally, duplicates should be excluded from candidate generation while raw AWL membership remains preserved for provenance and research.

## Current database status

The operational sequencing\_ladder remains unchanged at 10,381 WordNet V2 sense placements. No production Bathmonym levels have been rewritten.  
A non-destructive v0.2.1 preview now contains all 16,751 learner-active candidate senses. These correspond to 6,319 distinct lemma strings. Therefore, “16,751” does not mean 16,751 different written words; it is a pre-pruning pool of meanings.  
Preview counts by proposed level:

* L1 116; L2 1,488; L3 2,358; L4 1,740; L5 1,713;  
* L6 1,135; L7 1,540; L8 2,080; L9 2,120; L10 2,461.

The preview includes 200 deterministic review samples, 20 from each level. It is not yet a teach-ready final list. The uneven level sizes are evidence, not a defect to hide: levels should be shaped by pedagogical need, not forced into equal buckets.

## Placement rules in v0.2.1

* Sense-specific CEFR evidence is the strongest current anchor.  
* A2, B1, and B2 are split into their lower and plus bands by within-band teaching priority, using frequency and supporting evidence.  
* An unanchored lemma-level theme may drive placement only when the candidate is itself theme-sourced and has Commonality 1\. Otherwise the theme is advisory.  
* Unanchored NGSL senses receive a frequency-based provisional band; Commonality may delay them, but only as fallback evidence.  
* Unanchored NAWL senses receive a provisional L8–L10 placement by NAWL rank thirds.  
* No taxonomic parent-before-child rule changes a level automatically. Definitional and conceptual prerequisites will be modeled separately.

Every preview decision records its placement basis, confidence, flags, GSE interval, and reasoning. The earlier v0.2 run was preserved as superseded after a semantic spot-check found lemma-theme leakage; v0.2.1 records the correction.

## Correlation and validation

Among CEFR-linked senses, v0.2.1 has Pearson correlation 0.9781 and Spearman rank correlation 0.9795 between CEFR order and proposed Bathmonym level. This is expected because CEFR is intentionally the structural anchor. It shows synchronization, not independent proof that every item is correctly placed.  
Pearson correlation measures linear association. Spearman correlation measures whether the rank order rises consistently, even when spacing is not linear. Also, Pearson the education company and the Pearson correlation coefficient are unrelated uses of the same name.  
Independent validation must come next from young-learner evidence, Korean curriculum alignment, age of acquisition, concreteness, child-corpus dispersion, definition vocabulary, morphology, and teacher review.

## What “finished” should mean

A finished Bathmonym is not merely a merged list. It is a reversible, sense-aware teaching sequence in which each admitted meaning has one level, transparent evidence, and a review trail. The final number of admitted senses should be chosen after normalization and pruning; it should not be forced to 13,000 or 16,000 in advance.  
The immediate completion path is:

* normalize and sense-match the external child, curriculum, NGSL, NAWL, AWL, and Fry evidence;  
* add age-of-acquisition, concreteness, and school/child-corpus features;  
* review the 200-sense stratified sample and repair placement rules;  
* decide the admitted core boundary and prune duplicates, obscure senses, and pedagogically unhelpful meanings without deleting source provenance;  
* verify level counts, CEFR/GSE agreement, grade-stage behavior, and teacher usability before replacing the operational ladder.
