# Varcom2

VarCom: A Unified Theory of Communication as Variation-Based Meaning Transmission

Abstract

This paper introduces Variation Communication Theory (VarCom), a unified framework grounded in variation and meaning. Rather than defining communication in terms of intention, VarCom distinguishes between signaling and communication.

Signaling is the emission of meaningful variation.

Communication is the transmission of meaning.

Meaning is carried through variation and may be encoded (as in signals) or inferred (as in cues). This framework separates the production of variation from its transmission and interpretation, and removes dependence on intention or successful reception. VarCom integrates physical modalities, signal forms, encoding, and information flow into a unified ontology applicable across biological, technological, and environmental systems.

## **🔎 Addendum: Emission and Reception Modes**

VarCom defines signaling as the emission of meaningful variation and communication as the transmission of meaning. A further distinction can be made between **emission (sender-side)** and **reception (receiver-side)**.

### **1\. Emission (Sender-Side)**

Emission describes how signals are produced or persist:

* **Always-On (Persistent)**  
  Signals exist continuously without requiring a triggering event.  
  * Examples: frog coloration, billboards, continuous broadcasts  
* **Triggered (Event-Based)**  
  Signals are produced in discrete events.  
  * Examples: speech, sending a message, pressing play

---

### **2\. Reception (Receiver-Side)**

Reception describes how signals are accessed:

* **Passive Reception**  
  Signals are received without active seeking.  
  * Examples: overhearing speech, noticing a sign, seeing coloration  
* **Active Reception (On-Demand)**  
  The receiver intentionally seeks or retrieves meaning.  
  * Examples: reading a book, opening a file, searching, tuning in

---

### **💎 Key Insight**

> Emission and reception are independent dimensions: signals may be always-on or triggered, while reception may be passive or active.

---

### **🔥 Summary Matrix**

|  | Passive Reception | Active Reception |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Always-On Emission** | billboard, frog coloration | searching for a sign |
| **Triggered Emission** | overheard speech | calling, requesting, or playing content |

---

### **💥 Final Statement**

> Signals are characterized by how they are **emitted (always-on vs triggered)** and how they are **received (passive vs active)**.

**🔎 Addendum: Transmission Pathways (Direct and Artifact-Mediated)**

VarCom defines signaling as the emission of meaningful variation and communication as the transmission of meaning. A further distinction can be made regarding the **pathway** through which meaning is conveyed.

### **1\. Direct Transmission (Unmediated)**

Meaning is transmitted directly from one organism to another without intermediate artifacts.

* Path: organism → organism  
* Examples: speech, gesture, touch

---

### **2\. Artifact-Mediated Transmission (Real-Time)**

Meaning is transmitted through an intermediate artifact or system in real time.

* Path: organism → artifact/system → organism  
* Examples: phone calls, radio, rope signaling, live digital communication

---

### **3\. Stored Transmission (Deferred)**

Meaning is encoded into an artifact and accessed later, introducing a temporal separation between emission and reception.

* Path: organism → artifact → (time delay) → organism  
* Examples: books, recordings, messages, files

---

### **💎 Key Insight**

> Transmission may occur **directly**, **through artifacts in real time**, or **through artifacts across time via storage**.

---

### **💥 Final Statement**

> Meaning is transmitted either directly between organisms, through artifacts in real time, or through stored artifacts across time.

## **🔎 Addendum: Artifact Chains in Transmission**

Communication is often not mediated by a single artifact, but by a **chain of artifacts**, each transforming, storing, or transmitting the signal.

### **🔗 Artifact Chain Structure**

> organism → artifact → artifact → … → artifact → organism

Each artifact may:

* **store** (e.g., memory, database)  
* **transform** (e.g., encode/decode, compress)  
* **transmit** (e.g., network, radio)

---

### **🧠 Example: Text Message**

1. You (organism)  
2. Your phone (artifact: input \+ encoding \+ storage)  
3. Network systems (artifacts: transmission chain)  
4. Receiver’s phone (artifact: storage \+ decoding)  
5. Receiver (organism)

---

### **💎 Key Insight**

> Communication may involve **multiple artifacts in sequence**, forming a transmission chain across space and time.

---

### **🔥 Important Clarification**

* The **message itself is not an artifact**  
   → it is the **signal (meaningful variation)**  
* The **devices and systems are artifacts**  
   → they carry, store, and transform the signal

---

### **💥 Final Statement**

> Meaning may be conveyed through chains of artifacts, where each artifact participates in storing, transforming, or transmitting the signal between organisms.
