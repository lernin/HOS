## **Executive Summary**

A **hodonym** (from Greek *hodos*, path + *onym*, name) is the path between any two concepts in a hierarchy. Hodonyms may be simple vertical climbs along direct supernym/distonym lines, or they may be traversals that ascend to a common peak (akronym ð️) and descend to the target.

This document formalizes the types, properties, and applications of hodonyms within the Axionyms framework.

## **Part One: Definition**

A hodonym is the **sequence of concepts** traversed when moving from one concept to another.

  

## **Part Three: Hodonym Properties**

### **3.1 Direction**

Hodonyms are inherently directional. The path from A to B may differ from the path from B to A only in reversal, but the **experience** of climbing vs descending may be conceptually distinct.

### **3.2 Length**

The length of a hodonym is the number of steps (edges) between concepts. Shorter hodonyms indicate closer conceptual relationships.

### **3.3 Peak Requirement**

  - **Vertical hodonyms** require no peak — they follow existing hierarchical lines
  - **Traversal hodonyms** require an **akronym** — a common ancestor where the paths meet

## **Part Four: The Hodonym Algorithm**

To find the hodonym between Concept A and Concept B:

### **Step 1: Check for Direct Thesonym Relationship**

text

if A.isSupernymOf(B):

    hodonym = \[A → ... → B\]  (downward)

elif B.isSupernymOf(A):

    hodonym = \[B → ... → A\]  (downward)

else:

    proceed to Step 2

### **Step 2: Find Common Akronym**

text

ancestorsA = A.getAllSupernyms()

ancestorsB = B.getAllSupernyms()

akronym = findLowestCommonAncestor(ancestorsA, ancestorsB)

### **Step 3: Construct Hodonym**

text

hodonym = \[A → ... → akronym → ... → B\]

## **Part Six: Practical Applications**

### **6.1 Semantic Distance**

The length of a hodonym provides a measure of conceptual distance. Shorter hodonym = closer relationship.

### **6.2 Teaching and Explanation**

When a learner knows Concept B but not Concept A:

1.  Compute hodonym from B to A
2.  Walk them along the path: "You know B. Let's go from there to here..."
3.  Each step is explainable

### **6.3 Knowledge Graph Navigation**

Hodonyms provide the natural routes through any knowledge graph — the paths that make sense for traversal, explanation, and discovery.

### **6.4 Curriculum Sequencing**

Learning progressions are hodonyms — paths from foundational concepts (hyporeonyms) to mastery targets (akronyms).

## **Part Eight: Pedagogical Note — The Mountain Analogy**

To understand hodonyms, **visualize a mountain range**.

  - **Vertical hodonyms** are like climbing a single mountain from base to peak — a direct path upward or downward.
  - **Traversal hodonyms** are like hiking from one mountain's base to another's — you must first climb to a ridge or peak that connects them, then descend.

The mountain is not the system. It is the **map** that helps you navigate it.

## **Conclusion**

Hodonyms are the paths through conceptual space. They connect what we know to what we seek to understand. Whether a simple climb or a journey over distant peaks, every hodonym tells a story of relationship.
