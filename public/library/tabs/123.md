# **Reclassifying Temporal Expressions as Noun-Based Anchors in English**

*A Functional and Ontological Argument for Time-Noun Treatment of “Yesterday,” “Today,” and “Tomorrow”*

---

## **Abstract**

Traditional grammar classifies words such as *yesterday*, *today*, and *tomorrow* as **adverbs of time** when they answer the question *“when?”*. This paper argues that such classification is pedagogically weak and ontologically inaccurate. Drawing on linguistic research (e.g., , ), we propose that these expressions are better understood as **temporal noun-like anchors**—elements that locate events within a conceptual space-time framework. This reinterpretation yields a clearer, more cognitively grounded model for language learners.

## **2\. Empirical Linguistic Support**

### **2.1 Temporal Expressions as Semantic Anchors**

(1987) shows that temporal expressions like *yesterday* function as:

**operators over time intervals**

Meaning:

* they don’t modify actions like manner adverbs  
* they **select a time region** in which the event occurs

---

### **2.2 Noun Phrases and Temporal Interpretation**

(1995) demonstrates:

* noun phrases can carry **temporal reference**  
* time can be encoded structurally like entities

Example:

* “The meeting yesterday was long”

Here:

* *yesterday* behaves like a **temporal noun modifier**  
* not a classic adverb

---

### **2.3 Cross-Linguistic Evidence**

Research across languages (Mandarin, Finnish, etc.) shows:

* “time nouns” and “time adverbs” behave differently syntactically  
* temporal expressions often:  
  * move like noun phrases  
  * anchor discourse

👉 Key idea:

Time expressions frequently behave like **reference points**, not modifiers.
