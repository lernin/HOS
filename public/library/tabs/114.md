* \#\# 1\. ANTOMONYM FOUNDATION  
*   
*   
* The hypernym/hyponym tree is not populated by ambiguous strings (words). It is populated by \*\*antomonyms\*\* — unique identifiers, each representing a disambiguated real kind.  
*   
*   
* A single string (e.g., "plane") may map to multiple antomonyms:  
* \- plane (antomonym: ANT-001) → hypernym: aircraft  
* \- plane (antomonym: ANT-002) → hypernym: geometric surface  
* \- plane (antomonym: ANT-003) → hypernym: carpentry tool  
*   
*   
* Each antomonym has:  
* \- a unique identifier  
* \- exactly one hypernym (its parent)  
* \- zero or more hyponyms (its children)  
* \- a canonical name (real-world category name)  
* \- associated attributes (color, interface, etc.)  
*   
*   
* \*\*Principle:\*\* Disambiguation precedes all analysis. The tree is a hierarchy of real kinds, not words.  
*

* \#\# 2\. BRAINSTORM (DISCOVERY)  
*   
*   
* Generate a wide, unstructured set of:  
* \- candidate types  
* \- possible axes  
* \- real-world examples  
*   
*   
* \*\*Goal:\*\* maximize coverage before filtering.  
*   
*   
* \---

*   
* \#\# 3\. TYPE GATE — ERD (EVENT–ROLE DISTRIBUTION)  
*   
*   
* For each candidate type:  
* \- sample real-world events involving the topic  
* \- for each event, does the candidate function as X? (yes/no)  
* \- estimate % distribution across events  
* \- apply threshold (\~30%)  
*   
*   
* \*\*Result:\*\*  
* \- PASS → continue  
* \- FAIL → reject as type  
*   
*   
* \---  
*   
*   
* \#\# 4\. CORE TYPE VALIDATION  
*   
*   
* A valid TYPE must change what the thing IS, based on:  
* \- \*\*structure\*\* — physical or formal organization  
* \- \*\*composition\*\* — material or constituent parts  
* \- \*\*causal mechanism\*\* — how it produces its effects  
* \- \*\*governing constraints\*\* — the primary laws or principles that determine its behavior  
* \- \*\*persistence / failure conditions\*\* — how it endures or breaks  
*   
*   
* If none of these differ between two things → they are not distinct types.  
*   
*   
* \*\*Reject the following as types:\*\*  
* \- descriptions (red, fast, loud)  
* \- states (broken, old, new)  
* \- roles/purposes (transportation device, writing tool)  
* \- context/location (indoor, outdoor, urban)  
* \- ownership (mine, yours, rental)  
* \- temporary conditions (wet, damaged, activated)  
*   
*   
* These may become attributes, but never types.  
*   
*   
* \---  
*   
*   
* \#\# 5\. GOVERNING CONSTRAINT PRIMACY  
*   
*   
* Determine the single most fundamental governing constraint for the concept.  
*   
*   
* Ask: \*"What one constraint determines the majority of the thing’s structure, composition, and behavior?"\*  
*   
*   
* \*\*Examples:\*\*  
* \- Vehicle → medium of operation (land, water, air, space)  
* \- Food → processing / structural transformation (unprocessed, processed, prepared, preserved)  
* \- Entity → physical vs abstract existence  
*   
*   
* This constraint becomes the root-level split candidate.  
*   
*   
* \---  
*

* \#\# 6\. AXIS DISCOVERY (N-DIMENSIONAL ARRAY)  
*   
*   
* During analysis:  
* 1\. Identify all possible axes (governing constraints, structural dimensions)  
* 2\. For each axis, identify its possible values  
* 3\. Populate the n-dimensional array to reveal all stable, real kinds at every intersection  
*   
*   
* The array is the source of truth for discovery. It shows which combinations of constraints yield real things in the world.  
*   
*   
* Do not finalize any hierarchy until the array is complete.  
*

* \---

* \#\# 7\. AXIS ASSIGNMENT  
*   
*   
* For each validated type:  
* \- Which axis values are essential?  
* \- Single match → simple type  
* \- Multiple matches → candidate hybrid  
*   
*   
* Ignore:  
* \- incidental behavior  
* \- rare cases  
*

* \#\# 8\. HYBRID RECOGNITION  
*   
*   
* A \*\*hybrid\*\* is a stable, real kind that occupies intersections across multiple axes.  
*   
*   
* \*\*Examples:\*\*  
* \- Amphibious vehicle — intersects land \+ water operation axes  
* \- Hovercraft — a specific kind of amphibious vehicle  
* \- Keyboard instrument — intersects chordophone, aerophone, electrophone axes via interface  
*   
*   
* \*\*Hybrids are not:\*\*  
* \- a failure to classify  
* \- a problem to be solved  
* \- something to force into an existing single-axis category  
*   
*   
* They are legitimate real kinds discovered through array analysis.

* 9\. EMERGENT KIND TEST — MEMBER-FORCED CATEGORY TEST (RECURSIVE)  
* A candidate category must be tested by examining its members.  
* Ask: "Do the members of this candidate category fit cleanly into existing categories, or do they force the creation of a new category because no existing category accommodates them?"  
* If members fit cleanly into existing categories → candidate is an attribute/facet (selection across categories)  
* If members do not fit into existing categories → candidate is an emergent kind and becomes a type  
* Recursive Application: After a new category is established, test again at the next level. For each category, examine its members. Group them by governing constraints (structure, causal mechanism, etc.). If members within the category split into distinct families that share a coherent intermediate kind, promote that intermediate category. Repeat recursively until all members fit cleanly into their parent category without forcing distortion.  
* Test Case — Amphibious vehicle (recursive):  
* Members: wheeled amphibian, tracked amphibian, hovercraft, ekranoplan  
* At Level 1: Do they fit cleanly into land vehicle or watercraft? No → emergent kind under multi-mode vehicle  
* At Level 2: Do all members fit cleanly into amphibious vehicle as a single category?  
* No — members split into two families with different governing constraints  
* Contact amphibians (wheeled, tracked): dual propulsion, displacement hull  
* Ground effect amphibians (hovercraft, ekranoplan): air cushion/ground effect, non-contact  
* Verdict: Promote intermediate categories (contact amphibian, ground effect amphibian) as direct hyponyms under amphibious vehicle

* \#\# 10\. ATTRIBUTE RULE  
*   
*   
* An attribute is a distinction that does \*\*not\*\* change:  
* \- structure  
* \- composition  
* \- causal mechanism  
* \- governing constraints  
* \- persistence / failure conditions  
*   
*   
* \*\*Attributes are:\*\*  
* \- valid for querying, filtering, and faceting  
* \- not represented as type nodes in the hierarchy  
* \- applied to antomonyms after disambiguation  
*   
*   
* \*\*Examples:\*\*  
* \- color (red, blue)  
* \- control interface (keyboard, touch)  
* \- location (indoor, outdoor)  
* \- material (wood, metal) — when not a structural distinction  
*   
*   
* \---  
*   
*   
* \#\# 11\. TYPE vs. ATTRIBUTE DECISION TEST  
*   
*   
* For each candidate kind discovered in the array:  
*   
*   
* | Criteria | Type | Attribute |  
* |----------|------|-----------|  
* | Changes structure/composition | ✓ | ✗ |  
* | Changes causal mechanism | ✓ | ✗ |  
* | Defines governing constraint | ✓ | ✗ |  
* | Changes persistence/failure | ✓ | ✗ |  
* | Members force new category (no clean existing home) | ✓ | ✗ |  
* | Members fit cleanly into existing categories | ✗ | ✓ |  
* | Is a temporary condition | ✗ | ✓ |  
*   
*   
* If the candidate meets type criteria → becomes a type node.  
* If the candidate meets attribute criteria → becomes an attribute facet.

* \#\# 12\. STRUCTURE RULES  
*   
*   
* \#\#\# SINGLE INHERITANCE RULE  
* Every antomonym has exactly one parent.  
* \- No polyhierarchy  
* \- No multiple inheritance  
* \- No "exists in two places"  
*   
*   
* Hybrids that pass the Emergent Kind Test (members do not fit existing categories) are placed under one parent — the parent determined by their primary governing constraint or the coherent intermediate category they form.  
*   
*   
* \#\#\# DIRECTNESS RULE  
* Insert missing intermediate layers. Never skip levels.  
*   
*   
* If a kind has subtypes that share a coherent intermediate category, promote that category as a direct hyponym before listing its children.  
*   
* RECURSIVE DIRECTNESS RULE  
* After establishing any category, examine its members. Group them by governing constraints. If members form coherent families that share structure, mechanism, or medium, promote those families as intermediate categories before listing their children. Apply recursively until all members fit cleanly into their parent.

* 13\. CANONICAL NAMING RULE  
* Use real-world category names—the terms people actually use.  
* Prefer:  
* standalone nouns when they exist and are commonly used (aircraft, marsupial, corkscrew)  
* established expert terms where appropriate  
* simpler, widely understood terms when they capture the distinction cleanly  
* Accept multi-word names when:  
* no single-word term exists in common usage (sports equipment, parking garage, dental floss)  
* the multi-word name is what people actually say (placental mammal, delivery truck, coffee table)  
* the parent repetition clarifies rather than merely repeats (sports equipment is equipment; "sports" alone is not a type of equipment)  
* Avoid:  
* unnecessary technical terms when simpler language suffices  
* invented labels  
* descriptive phrases when an established name already exists  
* forced single-word names that no one uses  
* Principle: The name should reflect real-world usage. If people say "placental mammal," use "placental mammal." If people say "marsupial," use "marsupial." The goal is recognition and naturalness, not brevity at the cost of clarity.

* \#\# 14\. SIGNIFICANCE & SUFFICIENCY RULE  
*   
*   
* Direct types must be:  
* \- \*\*True\*\* — real kinds, not mental groupings  
* \- \*\*Relevant\*\* — belong under the concept  
* \- \*\*Significant\*\* — important enough to list (cultural prominence, structural distinctiveness, user expectation)  
* \- \*\*Sufficient\*\* — the list is complete enough for the intended purpose; stop when additional types would be excessive or minor  
*   
*   
* Do not list every true and relevant kind. Apply significance filtering to avoid clutter and maintain clarity.

* \#\# 15\. EXHAUSTIVE TESTING LOOP  
*   
*   
* Before finalizing any split:  
*   
*   
* 1\. Generate the candidate set using the current axis model  
* 2\. Test exhaustively: For each stable, real kind in the domain, does it fit cleanly into one candidate? If not, which kinds are unaccounted for?  
* 3\. Identify escapees: Kinds that don't fit are evidence that:  
*    \- the axis set is incomplete  
*    \- a hybrid category is required  
*    \- the candidate split is wrong  
* 4\. Iterate: Add axes, adjust, or promote hybrids until all stable kinds are accounted for  
* 5\. Do not finalize until the candidate set accounts for all significant, stable kinds without forcing misfits  
*   
*   
* This replaces passive convergence with active, exhaustive testing.

* \#\# 16\. CONVERGENCE LOOP  
*   
*   
* Repeat the entire sequence until the result is:  
* \- \*\*minimal\*\* — no unnecessary nodes  
* \- \*\*stable\*\* — consistent across use cases  
* \- \*\*non-overlapping\*\* — no sibling categories that overlap in meaning  
* \- \*\*reality-grounded\*\* — every node corresponds to a real kind in the world  
* \- \*\*canonically named\*\* — using appropriate, clear terminology  
*   
*   
* \*\*The loop iterates through:\*\*

* \#\# FINAL CHECK  
*   
*   
* Each type must be:  
* \- structurally/mechanistically distinct  
* \- not role/context-based  
* \- a real kind (represented by an antomonym)  
* \- derived from the most fundamental governing constraint(s)  
* \- validated by the Emergent Kind Test — members do NOT fit cleanly into existing categories  
* \- named using clear, appropriate terms  
* \- significant enough to list  
* \- sufficient to complete the level  
* \- accounted for in the exhaustive test — no forced fits  
* \- single parent — no polyhierarchy
