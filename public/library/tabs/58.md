Love this direction, Ashley. You’re right: **missions ≠ only learning**. They’re *guided attention*. For leaders, that’s setup, reflection, intention.

Here’s a clean, **Phase-later but design-now** list of **Leader interview / mission prompts**. Think of these as *gentle, skippable, dignity-preserving* prompts — not homework.

## **School Lead — Foundational Missions (Interview-style)**

### **Identity & Direction**

  - “How would you describe your school in one sentence?”
  - “What kind of students do you most want to help?”
  - “What problem does your school exist to solve?”
  - “What should parents feel when they walk in?”

### **Philosophy & Values**

  - “What matters more here: speed or depth?”
  - “How do you feel about mistakes in learning?”
  - “Is discipline more about structure or trust?”
  - “What does ‘success’ look like for your students?”

### **Learning Approach**

  - “What do you believe about memorization vs understanding?”
  - “What role should homework play?”
  - “How important is test performance here?”
  - “How do you feel about AI in learning?”

### **Culture & Tone**

  - “Three words that describe your school’s atmosphere”
  - “Formal or warm?”
  - “Strict or flexible?”
  - “Traditional or experimental?”

### **Differentiation**

  - “What makes your school different from others nearby?”
  - “Who is *not* a good fit for your school?”
  - “What do you do especially well?”

### **Parent Relationship**

  - “How involved should parents be?”
  - “How often should parents hear from teachers?”
  - “What do parents most misunderstand about learning?”

### **Staff & Teaching**

  - “What makes a great teacher *here*?”
  - “What behaviors are encouraged among staff?”
  - “What should teachers never feel afraid to say?”

### **Vision (Light, optional)**

  - “In 5 years, what do you hope people say about this school?”
  - “What would you change about education if you could?”

## **How this should** ***feel*** **in the product**

  - Framed as: **“Help us understand your school”**
  - Clearly optional
  - One question at a time
  - AI can:
      
      - paraphrase
      - summarize
      - draft a school motto
      - generate a philosophy page
  - Stored as **living context**, not static forms

## **Strong opinion (worth locking in)**

  - These are **missions**, not settings
  - They build *shared understanding*, not configuration
  - They make the system feel *thoughtful*, not bureaucratic

You’re onto something big here. This is how the product stops feeling like software and starts feeling like a partner.

## **What this actually is**

This is **leadership coaching**, disguised as onboarding.

The system is saying:

“You’re leading a school. Let’s help you clarify what you believe.”

Not:

  - “Fill this out”
  - “Configure settings”
  - “Do paperwork”

## **How it should be presented to the leader**

Tone:

  - Respectful
  - Curious
  - Non-judgmental

Language:

  - “Want to think this through together?”
  - “You can skip any of this.”
  - “This helps us support your school better.”

Positioning:

  - Appears **after** school creation
  - Appears **over time**, not all at once
  - One thoughtful question per session

## **Why this is powerful (and rare)**

Most systems:

  - Ask *what you do*
  - Ask *how many*
  - Ask *when*

Yours asks:

  - *Why*
  - *What you value*
  - *What you want to protect*

That’s leadership-level thinking.

## **What the system does with it (quietly)**

  - Shapes AI suggestions
  - Tunes tone of feedback
  - Aligns parent communication
  - Influences curriculum pacing later
  - Helps staff understand expectations

And importantly:

  - **Nothing is enforced**
  - **Nothing is locked**
  - **Everything evolves**
