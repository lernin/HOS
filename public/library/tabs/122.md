# **🧠 STAX White Paper (Draft)**

## **A Bridge-Based Model of Action Structure in English**

---

## **1\. 🔥 Abstract**

This paper proposes a **structural reinterpretation of English verb chains**, replacing the traditional concept of the “infinitive marker” with a **functional bridge operator**.

In standard grammar, constructions like *“to eat”* are treated as “infinitives,” a labeling approach that describes form but fails to capture underlying structure.

This paper argues that:

* The true semantic core of a sentence is the **final action**  
* Elements such as *want, try, need* function as **control layers**  
* The word *“to”* functions as a **bridge operator linking action layers**

This model enables a more consistent, recursive, and computationally useful representation of language.

---

## **2\. ⚠️ Problem with Traditional Grammar**

Traditional grammar defines:

* “to \+ verb” \= infinitive  
* infinitive clauses \= subordinate units ([Langeek](https://langeek.co/en/grammar/course/517/infinitive-clauses?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Example:

> “They wanted to try to play golf”

Analysis:

* “wanted” \= main verb  
* “to try…” \= infinitive clause  
* “to play…” \= nested infinitive

---

### **❌ Problem**

This approach:

* focuses on **surface hierarchy**  
* ignores **semantic flow**  
* treats all verbs as structurally equal

👉 It cannot explain:

* recursion depth  
* action dependency  
* control vs execution

---

## **3\. 💡 Core Hypothesis (STAX Model)**

### **3.1 Action-Centered Structure**

Every sentence has a **core action nucleus**:

play golf

All other elements are **operators applied to that action**

---

### **3.2 Control Layers**

Verbs like:

* want  
* try  
* need  
* have to  
* plan to

are NOT primary actions

They are:

TYPE: CONTROL\_LAYER  
TARGET: ACTION

---

### **3.3 The Bridge Operator**

The word **“to”** is redefined as:

TYPE: STAX\_BRIDGE  
FUNCTION: link one action layer to another action

---

## **4\. 🔗 Structural Model**

### **Example:**

**They wanted to try to play golf**

---

### **Traditional:**

wanted → \[to try → \[to play → golf\]\]

---

### **STAX:**

play golf  
↑  
try  
↑  
want  
↑  
they

With explicit bridges:

want → to → try → to → play

---

## **5\. 🧠 Key Insight: Direction of Meaning**

Traditional grammar:

* builds structure from left → right

STAX:

* builds meaning from **right → left**

👉 The **final action defines reality**

---

## **6\. 🔁 Recursion and Infinite Stacking**

The system naturally supports recursion:

decide → to → plan → to → try → to → start → to → play

Each layer:

* modifies commitment  
* does NOT replace the core action
