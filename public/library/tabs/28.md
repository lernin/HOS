\# Procedia Ontology: The Five Categories

  

\*\*Author:\*\* Ashley

\*\*Date:\*\* March 2026

\*\*Version:\*\* 2.0 — Complete Reference

\*\*Purpose:\*\* A human-scale ontology for knowledge representation, integrating core categories, subcategory hierarchies, grammar mapping, relationship types, and Procedia implementation.

  

\---

  

\#\# 1. Core Insight

  

Everything that exists — or that we need to talk about — falls into exactly five categories. These map to how humans experience reality, how grammar works, and how a database is structured.

  

| Category | Greek Root | Meaning | Core Question |

|----------|------------|---------|---------------|

| \*\*Autonym\*\* | αὐτός (self) + ὄνυμα (name) | self‑named | What is it? |

| \*\*Synergonym\*\* | σύν (with) + ἔργον (work) + ὄνυμα (name) | co‑working‑named | What is it like? What does it have? |

| \*\*Genonym\*\* | γίγνομαι (become, happen) + ὄνυμα (name) | becoming‑named | What happens? |

| \*\*Morphonym\*\* | μορφή (form) + ὄνυμα (name) | form‑named | What pattern does it follow? |

| \*\*Horonym\*\* | ὅρος (boundary) + ὄνυμα (name) | boundary‑named | What group does it belong to? |

  

These five are mutually exclusive at the primary level, though a single entity may participate in multiple categories through different relationships (e.g., a book is an Autonym physically, but its content is a Synergonym and its structure a Morphonym).

  

\---

  

\#\# 2. The Five Categories in Detail

  

\#\#\# 2.1 Autonym — Self‑named

  

\*\*Definition:\*\* Entities that can stand alone. They do not require anything else to exist first.

  

\*\*Subcategory Hierarchy:\*\*

  

\`\`\`

Autonym (Independent Thing)

├── Living Being

│   ├── Person (student, teacher, Ashley)

│   ├── Animal (dog, cat, scorpion)

│   └── Plant (tree, flower)

├── Natural Object (rock, river, mountain, star, ocean)

├── Structure (building, bridge, wall)

├── Infrastructure (power grid, railway)

├── Place (city, room, courtyard)

├── Fixture (sink, door, lighting)

├── Furniture (chair, table)

├── Equipment (projector, lab equipment)

├── Tool (hammer, screwdriver)

├── Device (phone, telescope)

├── Vehicle (car, airplane)

├── Container (box, bottle)

└── Product (book, clothing)

\`\`\`

  

\*\*Grammar:\*\* Nouns — subjects and objects of sentences.

  

\*\*Procedia tables:\*\* \`persons\`, \`devices\`, \`schools\` (as physical buildings), \`sites\`, \`spaces\`

  

\*\*Test:\*\* Can it exist alone? → Autonym

  

\---

  

\#\#\# 2.2 Synergonym — Co‑working‑named

  

\*\*Definition:\*\* Entities that cannot exist on their own. They must attach to or inhere in an Autonym. These are complements — they complete, describe, or modify.

  

\*\*Subcategory Hierarchy:\*\*

  

\`\`\`

Synergonym (Dependent Thing)

├── Material (wood, water, metal)

├── Supply (paper, ink, fabric)

├── Ingredient (flour, sugar, pigment)

├── Component (screw, wheel, handle, leg)

├── Role (teacher, parent, leader, student)

├── Relation (mother‑of, part‑of, owns, attends)

├── Emotion (fear, joy, confusion, love)

├── Social Construct (money, law, institution)

├── State (broken, open, hungry, tired, active, pending)

├── Measurement (5kg, 3 meters, level 4)

├── Quality (red, tall, heavy, friendly, loyal, conductive)

├── Capability (can reset PIN, \`school.classroom.\*\`)

└── Information Content (a sentence, a concept, a melody)

\`\`\`

  

\*\*Grammar:\*\* Adjectives, predicate complements, predicate nominatives, prepositional phrases, dependent nouns.

  

\*\*Procedia tables:\*\* \`roles\`, \`capabilities\`, \`relationships\`, \`enrollments\`, \`positions\`, \`person\_information\`, \`pins\`, \`person\_connections\`

  

\*\*Test:\*\* Does it depend on something else to exist? → Synergonym

  

\---

  

\#\#\# 2.3 Genonym — Becoming‑named

  

\*\*Definition:\*\* Events, processes, and actions that unfold. They have beginnings and ends.

  

\*\*Subcategory Hierarchy:\*\*

  

\`\`\`

Genonym (Happening)

├── Event

│   ├── Social Event (lesson, concert, birthday party, wedding)

│   ├── Emergency (earthquake, fire, medical emergency)

│   └── Natural Event (rainstorm, sunrise, earthquake)

├── Process (learning, cooking, growing, healing, construction)

├── Action (writing, teaching, speaking, jogging, hammering)

└── Duration (class period, semester, meeting length)

\`\`\`

  

\*\*Grammar:\*\* Verbs and verbal nouns.

  

\*\*Procedia tables:\*\* \`lessons\`, \`emergency\_events\`, \`sessions\`, \`actions\`

  

\*\*Test:\*\* Does it happen? Does it have a beginning and end? → Genonym

  

\*\*Note on scale:\*\* A Genonym can contain other Genonyms through \`has‑part\` relations:

\- \*Birthday party\* (event) \`has‑part\` \*cake cutting\* (activity)

\- \*Cake cutting\* (activity) \`has‑part\` \*blow candles\* (action)

  

The same thing (yoga) can be an event (60‑minute class), an activity (part of a workout), or an action (a single sun salutation) depending on context. The model uses role qualifiers on relationships, not separate types.

  

\---

  

\#\#\# 2.4 Morphonym — Form‑named

  

\*\*Definition:\*\* The shape, structure, or measurement of how things change over time. Not the event itself — its pattern.

  

\*\*Subcategory Hierarchy:\*\*

  

\`\`\`

Morphonym (Pattern)

├── Process Profile (mastery curve, forgetting curve, stability over time)

├── Temporal Pattern (schedule, routine, daily rhythm)

├── Measurement Series (temperature chart, heart rate graph, engagement over time)

├── Information Structure (document format, musical score, lesson plan)

├── Conceptual Rule (grammar rule, mathematical function, logical structure)

└── Time as Measurement (3:00pm, January, 2026)

\`\`\`

  

\*\*Grammar:\*\* Adverbs, measurements, structural descriptions.

  

\*\*Procedia tables:\*\* \`concept\_mastery.stability\` (over time), \`schedules\`, spaced repetition intervals, analytics dashboards

  

\*\*Test:\*\* Is it a pattern, shape, or structure? → Morphonym

  

\---

  

\#\#\# 2.5 Horonym — Boundary‑named

  

\*\*Definition:\*\* Boundaries we draw. The contents are real, but the container exists by agreement or conceptual partition.

  

\*\*Subcategory Hierarchy:\*\*

  

\`\`\`

Horonym (Boundary)

├── Social Group (home, section, cohort, team, class, club)

├── Institution (school as organization, company, government)

├── Geographic Boundary (school district, service area, neighborhood, "north of the river")

├── Conceptual Category (genre, subject area, department, grade level)

├── Role Set ("the teachers", "the 3rd graders", "the admin team")

└── Temporal Boundary (semester, Q1, "the 2025 cohort", "this semester")

\`\`\`

  

\*\*Grammar:\*\* Prepositions (\*in, within, among\*), collective nouns.

  

\*\*Procedia tables:\*\* \`homes\`, \`sections\`, \`cohorts\`, \`schools\` (as institutions), \`terms\`

  

\*\*Test:\*\* Is it a container defined by a boundary? → Horonym

\#\# 3. Grammar Mapping (The Language Connection)

  

This mapping shows that the ontology is not arbitrary — it reflects deep structures in language itself.

  

| Category | Grammatical Role | Example Sentence |

|----------|------------------|------------------|

| \*\*Autonym\*\* | Subject / Object | \*The hammer is on the table.\* |

| \*\*Synergonym\*\* | Complement / Modifier | \*The hammer is heavy. She is a teacher.\* |

| \*\*Genonym\*\* | Verb | \*She hammered the nail.\* |

| \*\*Morphonym\*\* | Adverb / Measurement | \*She works quickly. The class meets at 3pm.\* |

| \*\*Horonym\*\* | Prepositional phrase | \*Put it in the toolbox. She's on the team.\* |

\#\# 4. Relationship Types (The "Has" Dimensions)

  

Entities relate to each other through fundamental relationship types. Each relationship can have its own ID and carry qualifiers.

  

| Relationship | Question | Example | Category |

|--------------|----------|---------|----------|

| \`has‑part\` | What is it made of? | dog → leg | Meronymy |

| \`has‑quality\` | What is it like? | dog → friendly | Attribution |

| \`has‑role\` | What function does it serve? | person → teacher | Role‑bearing |

| \`has‑location\` | Where is it? | book → shelf | Spatial |

| \`has‑member\` | Who belongs to it? | team → player | Membership |

| \`has‑instance\` | What exemplifies it? | "dog" → Bailey | Instantiation |

| \`has‑property\` | What characteristics? | metal → conductive | Property |

| \`has‑capability\` | What can it do? | user → reset‑pin | Capability |

| \`has‑participant\` | Who takes part? | party → Ashley | Participation |

| \`has‑activity\` | What happens inside? | party → cake cutting | Containment |

  

\#\#\# Implementation

  

All relationships are stored in a unified \`relations\` table with:

\- \`subject\_id\` (the thing that has)

\- \`predicate\` (the type of relation)

\- \`object\_id\` (the thing that is had)

\- \`relation\_type\` (for indexing/filtering)

\- \`id\` (primary key — the relationship itself has an ID)

  

\#\#\# Qualifiers

  

Qualifiers attach to relationship IDs, allowing any relationship to carry unlimited metadata.

  

| relation\_id | qualifier\_type | value |

|-------------|----------------|-------|

| 101 | count | 4 |

| 201 | degree | very |

| 301 | sequence | 3 |

\#\# 5. Templates (Concepts vs. Instances)

  

The system distinguishes between general concepts and specific instances.

  

| Level | Example | Characteristics |

|-------|---------|-----------------|

| \*\*Concept (template)\*\* | "birthday party" | Has template relations: \`typically‑has‑participant\` (birthday child), \`typically‑has‑activity\` (cake cutting), \`typically‑uses‑object\` (cake) |

| \*\*Instance\*\* | "Ashley's 8th birthday" | Inherits template relations; can override (location = Chuck E. Cheese instead of home); adds specific qualifiers (candles count = 8) |

  

Both are stored in the same tables. The difference is an \`is\_template\` flag or a link to a \`template\_id\`.

  

\---

  

\#\# 6. The Undifferentiation Principle

  

\*\*Guideline:\*\* Differentiate only when the system behaves differently. Otherwise, let relationships carry the meaning.

  

\#\#\# Applied to events/activities/actions:

\- All are \*\*Genonyms\*\*

\- Scale is captured by \`has‑part\` relations and role qualifiers

\- No separate tables for "activity" vs "event" vs "action"

  

\#\#\# Applied to parts/qualities/roles:

\- All are \*\*Synergonyms\*\*

\- Distinguished by predicate (\`has‑part\`, \`has‑quality\`, \`has‑role\`)

\- No separate tables for each type

  

This principle keeps the ontology lean while maintaining expressive power.

\#\# 7. BFO Alignment

  

The Procedia five-category ontology aligns with the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) upper ontology as follows:

  

\`\`\`

Entity

├── Independent Thing (Autonym)

├── Dependent Thing (Synergonym)

├── Happening (Genonym)

├── Pattern (Morphonym)

└── Boundary (Horonym)

\`\`\`

  

This alignment ensures compatibility with established ontological frameworks while maintaining the human-scale simplicity of the five-category model.

\#\#\# Why Only Five?

  

1\. \*\*Match human experience\*\* — people naturally think in terms of things, qualities, events, patterns, and groups.

2\. \*\*Map to grammar\*\* — nouns, complements, verbs, adverbs, prepositions. Language already encodes this.

3\. \*\*Mutually exclusive\*\* — everything has one primary category, even if it touches others.

4\. \*\*Cover your domain\*\* — every Procedia table fits cleanly.

5\. \*\*More would blur\*\* — six categories would force distinctions that don't help building.

6\. \*\*Less would miss\*\* — four would force something important into the wrong bucket.
