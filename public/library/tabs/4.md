# **Master Spec v2.4 (Coding Spec)**

## **0) Core mental model**

We model **people**, **homes**, and **schools**.

  - **Home** = the private grouping that supports:
      
      - recovery (shared trust network)
      - managing private personal data for members (and granting visibility to schools via policy)
      - emergency policy + contacts
  - **School** = the institutional grouping that supports:
      
      - recovery (shared trust network)
      - enrollments (students at the school)
      - positions (teachers/admin/directors/etc.)
      - sections (teacher/student memberships + schedule)
      - capabilities (function access)

**Important UX framing:** Users feel like they’re “adding people” (to home) and “joining/connecting” (to school). They are *not* thinking “my home changed IDs.” Merge logic is backend-only.

**0.1 Phased architecture (doc-only framing)****  
  
**

  - **Phase 0 — Existence & Ground Truth:** persons, homes, devices, schools/sites/spaces, enrollments/positions, sections, schedules, lessons, artifacts can exist.
  - **Phase 1 — Connective Backbone:** connection\_codes + person\_connections normalize real-world linking; merges/confirmations happen later.
  - **Phase 2 — Participation & Ritual:** attendance/check-ins + karma (optional).
  - **Phase 3 — Learning Surface: The active learning layer where practice generates evidence and evidence updates mastery.   Queue → Missions → Practice → Concept Judgment → Mastery     Mastery is global per person and independent of school ownership.**

### **Evidence → Judgment → Mastery Flow**

1.  A learner completes a task (writing, speaking, quiz, etc.).  
      
2.  An artifact is created (raw submission).  
      
3.  One or more concept\_judgment rows are recorded.  
      
4.  The corresponding concept\_mastery row is updated.  
      
5.  Stability determines the next review date.  
      

## **Stability & Spacing Model**

Stability represents the durability of memory for a specific:

  - person  
      
  - concept  
      
  - facet  
      
  - lane (recognition | production)  
      

It is stored in:

concept\_mastery.stability

Stability maps to a review interval:

0 → 1 day  
 1 → 2 days  
 2 → 5 days  
 3 → 7 days  
 4 → 14 days  
 5 → 30 days  
 6 → 90 days  
 7 → 180 days  
 8 → 365 days

Review scheduling rule:

due\_at = last\_at + interval(stability)

## **Stability Update Rules**

When a new concept\_judgment is recorded:

If correct:

  - stability increases by 1 (capped at max tier)  
      

If incorrect:

  - stability decreases by 1 (or resets to lower tier per policy)  
      

Then:

  - last\_at is updated  
      
  - due\_at is recalculated  
      

Stability changes only through judgments.  
 Time alone does not change stability.

## **Table Responsibilities**

concept\_judgment  
 → stores historical evaluation events (correct/incorrect, strength, rung)

concept\_mastery  
 → stores current state (stability, due\_at, confidence)

artifacts  
 → store raw submission data

  
  

## **1) Identity and Authentication**

We use a **progressive friction model**.

Anyone may enter the system and begin using it immediately, without account-level authentication.  
 Identity verification is required **only** when a user attempts actions that involve authority, persistence, recovery, or access to protected data.

  - Students, Teachers, and Parents may begin without authentication and verify later if needed.
  - Admins and Leaders must authenticate **before** performing privileged or institutional actions.  
      

### **PINs (Safety, not identity)**

PINs are a **contextual safety tool**, not an identity requirement.

  - A PIN may be required when:
      
      - a device is shared,
      - multiple profiles exist on the same device,
      - performing actions that affect persistence, recovery, or other people.
  - On trusted personal devices or low-risk home use, a PIN may not be required.
  - PINs protect **profiles and lessons**, not identity itself.  
      

## **1.1 Person Record Creation Timing**

Selecting **Begin** begins an onboarding flow but does **not** immediately create a person record.

A durable person record is created **only after the user completes the onboarding decision tree and is routed into their first real experience**, such as:

  - Desk
  - Classroom
  - Office
  - Dashboard
  - Home  
      

At that moment:

  - the person record becomes persistent,
  - related records (home, memberships, positions, capabilities) are created as needed,
  - the identity is reused across future lessons and devices.  
      

## **1.1.5 Design Principle: Invisible System / Frictionless Interaction**

The system must prioritize **immediate user interaction** over all background processing.

  - **Critical path (frontstage):**
      
      - Render UI
      - Enable interaction
      - Respond instantly
  - **Non-critical work (backstage):**
      
      - Must be deferred, asynchronous, and non-blocking
      - Executed during idle time or low-frequency intervals
      - Never delays or interrupts the user experience

**Rules:**

  - Do not perform heavy computation or data collection on initial load
  - Do not ask users for information prematurely
  - Learn system state (devices, displays, patterns) **observationally over time**
  - Prompt users only when confidence is high and the action adds clear value

**Guiding principle:**

The user should never wait for bookkeeping.

The system should feel:

  - **instant at first use**
  - **intelligent over time**

## **1.2 Fast Entry and Resilience**

We prioritize a fast, uninterrupted UI.

  - Collect minimal information locally (for example, preferred name).
  - Route the user immediately into their initial experience.
  - Create the person record and related data **in the background**.
  - If background creation fails:
      
      - the local lesson remains active,
      - retries occur automatically,
      - the user is not blocked or kicked out of flow.  
          

## **1.3 Initial Routing vs Ongoing Access**

Onboarding determines an **initial landing space**, not long-term permissions.

  - Student → Desk
  - Teacher → Classroom
  - Admin → Office
  - Leader / Private Teacher → Dashboard (or Classroom, by product choice)

Initial routing is **not a restriction**.

Ongoing access is governed exclusively by **capabilities**:

  - Tabs or navigation links appear only for spaces the user has permission to enter.
  - Users with broad capabilities (e.g. school.\*) may freely move between Dashboard, Office, and Classroom after onboarding.

## **2.2 Connection Entry (Codes / QR / Handshake)**

**What this is****  
** Connection Entry is a relationship-directed entry path.  
 A person enters or links by using a **connection code** (QR or typed) instead of arriving alone.

Typical actions:

  - Scan a QR **or** enter a code  
      
  - Join another person, device, or screen  
      
  - Optionally record a relationship (“Who is this person to you?”)  
      

**Connection Codes****  
** Connection codes are temporary or reusable tokens stored in connection\_codes.

They are used for:

  - **Person connections** (family, friends, teachers, students)  
      
  - **Device pairing** (shared tablets, kiosks)  
      
  - **Screen joins** (classroom display, presentation screen)  
      

The **purpose** field (person\_connection | device\_pairing | screen\_join) determines how the code is interpreted.

A code may:

  - Have a giver (code\_giver\_id) or be system-generated  
      
  - Expire  
      
  - Be limited by max uses  
      

**Directionality (clear + explicit)****  
** Every successful use of a connection code creates a person\_connections row with:

  - **code\_giver** — the person who generated or displayed the code  
      
  - **code\_taker** — the person who scanned or entered the code  
      

The relationship is stored as:

giver\_relationship\_to\_taker

This removes all ambiguity about who is the parent, teacher, friend, etc.

**Source Context****  
** source\_context records *where* the connection happened, not what it grants.

Examples:

  - onboarding  
      
  - home  
      
  - school  
      
  - classroom  
      
  - device  
      
  - screen  
      
  - link / qr / code  
      

This is informational and audit-friendly only.

**Important rule****  
** Connection Entry **never grants roles, permissions, or school authority**.

All authority is resolved later through:

  - home membership  
      
  - enrollments  
      
  - staff positions  
      
  - capability grants  
      

**Unattributed entry****  
** If a person arrives via the website without a code:

  - They proceed through **Solo Entry****  
      
    **
  - No person\_connections record is created

## **4) Home merge and “adding people”**

The system supports low-friction onboarding that may temporarily create duplicate structures.  
 All merge behavior is **backend-only** and never exposed in the UI.

Users experience merges only as:

“You added X.”

### **4.1 Signup and connection modes**

We support two entry patterns that may later require merge resolution:

  - **Solo signup****  
    ** Creates a new home with a single member.  
      
  - **Connection signup****  
    ** A handshake between two people (or their homes) using a connection code.  
      

These paths are intentionally permissive to minimize friction.

### **4.2 Home merge principles**

Homes are merged only when a meaningful relationship is established.

Rules:

  - If a **guardian connects to a child**:  
      
      
      - The child joins the guardian’s home (preferred).  
          
  - If a guardian already exists and connects to a child who has a solo home:  
      
      
      - The child’s home is merged into the guardian’s home.  
          
  - If **siblings connect** and no guardian exists:  
      
      
      - Choose a deterministic winner home (e.g., earliest-created home or person).  
          
      - Merge the other home into it.  
          

A home may have **zero or one home lead**.  
 Home membership may change due to merges, but the UX never references IDs or “home changes.”

### **4.3 Home merge operation (backend)**

When merging **Home A** into **Home B**:

  - Reassign all home\_membership rows to Home B.  
      
  - Preserve all member relationships.  
      
  - Mark Home A as merged (soft-delete / archived).  
      
  - Maintain referential integrity.  
      

This process is not visible to users.

### **4.4 Person merge (deferred / non-MVP)**

It is possible for the same real-world child to be represented by multiple persons records due to independent entry paths  
 (e.g., a child joins a school first; later a parent creates a child via Home).

#### **Principles**

  - All persons records are structurally identical.  
      
  - “Placeholder” refers to a persons record with little or no activity; it is a **state**, not a distinct type.  
      

#### **Merge rule**

When two persons records are determined to represent the same child:

  - The record with **meaningful activity** is preserved  
     (artifacts, enrollments, section memberships, history).  
      
  - The record with **little or no activity** is merged into it.  
      
  - All foreign keys (e.g., home\_memberships, enrollments) are reassigned to the preserved record.  
      
  - The empty or low-activity record is archived or deleted.  
      

#### **Design intent**

  - Prevent duplicate long-term identities.  
      
  - Preserve student work and school history.  
      
  - Allow viral, low-friction onboarding without forcing early identity resolution.  
      

#### **Status**

  - Detection, tooling, and UI for person merges are **explicitly out of MVP scope**.  
      
  - This section documents intended future behavior only.

### **5) PINs, devices, and recovery**

#### **5.1 PIN basics**

  - PINs are stored as pin\_hash (never plaintext).  
      
  - Reusing the same PIN after a reset is allowed.  
      
  - PINs protect **profiles and lessons**, not identity.  
      

#### **5.2 Device entry vs reset (important distinction)**

  - **Re-add / Respark profile****  
    ** Re-adding a known profile to a device using:  
      
      
      - QR code or short code  
          
      - PIN confirmation  
         This is **lesson initiation**, not a reset.  
          
  - **PIN reset****  
    ** Used only when the PIN is forgotten or blocked.  
      

#### **5.3 Device types and authority**

A device is declared as one of:

  - personal  
      
  - home  
      
  - school  
      

**Home devices**

  - Driven by the home lead.  
      
  - Pre-populate only profiles approved by the home lead.  
      
  - Home members may re-enter without PIN (policy-controlled).  
      
  - Non-home members always require PIN.  
      

**School devices**

  - Driven by staff through **active lessons**, not ownership.  
      
  - No fixed or persistent roster.  
      
  - When a teacher starts or joins a lesson:  
      
      
      - The lesson’s section becomes the active context.  
          
      - All students in that section are temporarily available.  
          
      - Same-day additions are allowed.  
          
  - When the lesson ends, the roster clears.  
      

**Lesson-scoped rule**

  - A student may be active on only one device per lesson.  
      
  - Attempting to join another device:  
      
      
      - MVP: deny (or require explicit handoff later).

  

#### **5.3.5 Display Inference (Spec Section)**

## **Definition**

Displays are system-inferred entities representing physical screens associated with a device.

Displays are not user-created.

  

## **Fingerprint**

Each display is identified by:

screen\_width

screen\_height

device\_pixel\_ratio

  

**Observation Rule**

On stable load:

  - Capture fingerprint
  - Match existing display by fingerprint
  - If match:
      
      - observation\_count += 1
      - last\_seen\_at = now()
  - If no match:
      
      - Create new display with observation\_count = 1

  

## **Stability Threshold**

A display is considered **stable** when:

observation\_count ≥ N   (recommended: 5–10)

Only stable displays are used for system decisions.

  

## **Multi-Display Detection**

A device is multi-display if:

  - ≥ 2 displays meet stability threshold

  

## **Display Naming**

  - Disabled by default
  - Enabled only when multi-display is detected
  - Prompt is optional and non-blocking
  - display\_name is descriptive only (not used for identity)

  

## **Data Model**

displays

  - id (PK)
  - device\_id (FK → devices.id)
  - screen\_width
  - screen\_height
  - display\_name nullable   -- "Left Monitor", "TV", "Main Screen"
  - observation\_count (int, default 1)
  - last\_seen\_at
  - created\_at

  

## **Principles**

Displays are inferred, not declared

Confidence increases via repeated observation

User interaction is deferred until meaningful

  - **School members (students + staff)****

#### **5.5 PIN reset authority (who can initiate)**

A PIN reset may be initiated by:

  - Home primary (for members of that home).  
      
  - School staff with authority:  
      
      
      - School lead (director-level).  
          
      - Admins (default yes for MVP).  
          
      - Teachers **only if** they share an active section membership with the student.  
          

#### **5.6 PIN reset flow (Phase 0)**

**Core rule**

  - Phone number or email is never sufficient.  
      
  - A reset always requires a short-lived reset code approved by authority.  
      

**Known / trusted device**

1.  Person selects profile.  
      
2.  Enters PIN → taps “I forgot my PIN.”  
      
3.  System prompts to request reset code.  
      
4.  Home primary or authorized staff approves.  
      
5.  Person enters reset code.  
      
6.  Person sets new PIN.  
      
7.  Old PIN invalidated.  
      

**Unknown / new device**

1.  Person taps “Forgot PIN?”  
      
2.  System asks for identifier (lookup only).  
      
3.  System identifies target person.  
      
4.  Reset code approved by authority.  
      
5.  Person enters reset code.  
      
6.  Person sets new PIN.  
      

#### **5.7 Reset code security**

  - Short-lived (≈ 5 minutes).  
      
  - Single-use.  
      
  - Scoped to a specific target\_person\_id.  
      
  - Correct code alone does nothing unless paired with the correct person.  
      

#### **5.8 Non-MVP note: re-auth reminders**

  - Optional spaced re-auth:  
      
      
      - After N days, prompt for PIN.  
          
      - Failure routes to reset flow.  
          
      - Success extends interval.

  
  
  

## **6) Person data model: core person vs personal information**

The **persons** table represents a single global human identity.  
  

All personal, identifying, or sensitive data is stored **outside** the core person record and is governed by explicit access policy.

### **6.1 Core person (global identity)**

The persons table exists to answer one question:

“Who is this human, across all contexts?”

It contains only:

  - preferred\_name — what the person prefers to be called (UI default)  
      
  - user\_id (nullable, unique) — authentication linkage when/if present  
      
  - timestamps  
      

The persons record:

  - exists independently of any home or school  
      
  - attaches to all activity, learning evidence, and history  
      
  - must never be deleted once it has meaningful activity (only archived)  
      

No sensitive personal data lives here.

### **6.2 Personal information (owned by home, shared by policy)**

Personal and identifying data is stored in person\_information.

This may include (all optional):

  - legal\_name  
      
  - target\_language\_name (e.g. English name used in learning contexts)  
      
  - date\_of\_birth  
      
  - gender  
      
  - phone  
      
  - email  
      
  - important\_notes  
      
  - emergency\_notes  
      

Principles:

  - Personal information is **home-owned**, not school-owned  
      
  - Absence of data is normal and expected  
      
  - Schools never “collect” personal data into their own tables  
      

### **6.3 Visibility and access control**

School access to personal information is governed explicitly via  
 person\_information\_access.

Access is:

  - **scoped** (per enrollment or position)  
      
  - **field-level** (each data category is independently allowed)  
      
  - **revocable****  
      
    **
  - **auditable****  
      
    **

There is no implicit visibility:

  - Enrollment does **not** automatically expose personal data  
      
  - Staff roles do **not** imply visibility  
      
  - Capability grants control *functional access*, not personal data access  
      

### **6.4 Naming and display rules (UX guidance)**

Naming fields serve different purposes:

  - preferred\_name  
     → default UI name everywhere  
      
  - target\_language\_name  
     → commonly used in Korean education (e.g. English class name)  
      
  - legal\_name  
     → required only when explicitly needed (e.g. official enrollment)  
      

UI behavior:

  - Teachers may default to seeing target\_language\_name when permitted  
      
  - Legal name visibility requires explicit allowance  
      
  - UI may provide toggles where multiple names are permitted  
      

The system never assumes which name is “correct” — it respects context and consent.

  

## **7) Personal data ownership, classification, and visibility**

All personal data is **home-owned**.  
 Schools and other institutions may view personal data **only through explicit policy**, never by default.

There is no institutional intake, duplication, or shadow profiling of people.  
 The system maintains **one source of truth**, with controlled visibility.

### **7.1 Storage model (home-owned, single source of truth)**

All personal data is stored in **home-owned structures**, never in school-owned intake records. Information may be required for operational reasons, but **ownership never transfers**.

Primary storage:

  - person\_information  
      
      
      - legal\_name  
          
      - target\_language\_name  
          
      - date\_of\_birth  
          
      - gender  
          
      - phone  
          
      - email  
          
      - important\_notes  
          
      - emergency\_notes  
          

Supplementary home context:

  - homes.address  
      
  - emergency\_contacts (per home, prioritized)  
      

Principles:

  - Data may be partial, provisional, or added later  
      
  - Absence of data is normal and expected  
      
  - Schools never copy or persist personal data locally  
      

There are no profile blobs, duplicate forms, or school-side mirrors.

### **7.2 Important notes (daily-care relevant)**

**Important notes** are stored in:

  - person\_information.important\_notes  
      

These include information that:

  - is *not* an emergency declaration  
      
  - matters during normal daily interaction  
      
  - supports safe and informed care  
      

Examples:

  - epilepsy  
      
  - autism spectrum considerations  
      
  - fainting history  
      
  - narcolepsy  
      
  - severe allergies (non-emergency context)  
      
  - behavioral or learning considerations  
      

Visibility rules:

  - Important notes are **not visible by default****  
      
    **
  - Visibility is governed by person\_information\_access  
      
  - Schools may view these notes **only if explicitly allowed****  
      
    **
  - Access is scoped, revocable, and auditable  
      

Important notes support prevention and awareness, not crisis response.

### **7.3 Emergency-only notes (break-glass)**

**Emergency-only notes** are stored in:

  - person\_information.emergency\_notes  
      
  - emergency\_contacts  
      

These are intended **only for declared emergency situations**.

Rules:

  - Emergency notes are **never exposed** via enrollment or normal access policy  
      
  - They are accessible **only when an emergency is explicitly declared****  
      
    **
  - Emergency access must be:  
      
      
      - intentional  
          
      - time-bound  
          
      - attributable to a specific actor  
          
      - logged for audit  
          

Emergency notes exist for **break-glass scenarios only**.

### **7.4 Visibility control and access policy**

Institutional visibility of personal data is controlled through:

  - person\_information\_access  
      

Characteristics:

  - scoped to a specific enrollment or staff position  
      
  - field-level (each data category independently allowed)  
      
  - revocable at any time  
      
  - auditable  
      

Rules:

  - Enrollment does **not** imply personal data access  
      
  - Staff role does **not** imply personal data access  
      
  - Capabilities govern **actions**, not personal data visibility  
      

Personal data visibility is always:

  - explicit  
      
  - minimal  
      
  - context-aware  
      

### **7.5 Emergency declaration (invariant)**

An emergency declaration:

  - temporarily unlocks emergency-only data  
      
  - does not grant ongoing visibility  
      
  - must be logged with who / when / why  
      

Exact mechanics may evolve, but this invariant does not:

**Emergency data is sealed unless an emergency is declared.**

## **8) Schools (institution model)**

### **8.1 Rules**

A school is an organizational container with things like:

  - **enrollments** (student ↔ school)
  - **staff positions** (staff ↔ school)
  - **sections** (instructional group containers)
  - **sites** (physical / instructional locations)

Any person may be a student via enrollment (children or adults).  
 Roles are primarily **UX labels**; all real access is governed by **capabilities**.

### **8.2 Schools and sites**

A school may operate one or more **sites**, classified by sites.type, such as:

  - kindergarten
  - elementary\_school
  - middle\_school
  - high\_school
  - university
  - academy
  - tutor  
      

When an individual instructor starts independently:

  - create a **school** record
  - create a **site** with type = tutor
  - assign the instructor a **leader-level staff position**
  - grant full school capabilities by default  
      

This allows the instructor to:

  - create courses and sections
  - define schedules
  - run lessons
  - use administrative tools  
      

Additional staff, students, guardians, or sites may be added later.  
 No special flags or alternate models are required.

### **8.3 Staff positions**

A staff position connects a person to a school and is used for:

  - institutional staffing rosters
  - permission grants (capabilities)
  - auditability

### **8.4 Leaders and directors**

Leaders have broad access via capabilities:

  - dashboard
  - office
  - classroom  
     (typically granted as school.\*)  
      

There is no need for stacked roles (director + teacher + admin).  
 Capabilities are the source of truth.

## **9) School connections**

Two primary connection types:

### **9.1 Enrollments (student ↔ school)**

An enrollment says:

  - this person is a student at this school
  - contact\_person\_id defaults to home primary (if exists), but can be overridden

### **9.2 Staff positions (staff ↔ school)**

A staff position says:

  - this person works at this school
  - title/label may be customized (UI label: manager/teacher/coach/etc.)

  
  

## **10) sections (institutional model)**

### **10.1 Core idea**

A section is the unit of:

  - who is learning together
  - which teachers are involved
  - the schedule
  - classroom activity logs

### **10.2 section creation (teacher-first UX)**

When any user with classroom access clicks **“Add students”**:

  - if no section exists yet for this context, create a new section
  - add teacher membership for the creator
  - add student memberships for selected students

## **11) section memberships (replaces “roster”)**

We explicitly support many-to-many:

### **11.1 section student memberships**

Students can be in multiple sections at a school.

### **11.2 section teacher memberships**

Teachers can teach multiple sections.  
A section can have multiple teachers (assistant/substitute).

We avoid clunky “section teachers table where the same person appears 5 times” by embracing memberships.

## **12) Scheduling (Google Calendar-grade goal, MVP slice)**

Scheduling is a big system. We start simple but structure it like a real calendar.

### **12.1 MVP scheduling model (recommended)**

  - schedules (recurring definition)
      
      - day(s) of week
      - start time
      - duration
      - start date / optional end date
      - timezone
  - schedule\_overrides
      
      - cancellations
      - reschedules (make-up)
      - substitutions (teacher changes)
  - meeting\_instances
      
      - computed on the fly OR materialized as needed

### **12.2 Make-up lessons**

Two patterns:

1.  Override an existing instance (reschedule)
2.  Create an extra instance (additional meeting)

### **12.3 Open questions to resolve**

  - Do we materialize instances in DB or compute on demand?
  - How do we represent “holiday closures” at school level?
  - How do we handle substitute teacher for one instance?
  - When a student changes sections mid-term, what happens to future instances?

## **13) Personal Data Access Policies (school visibility)**

This is the “contract” allowing a school to view home-owned data.

### **13.1 Policy scope**

Policy attaches to:

  - enrollment (student at school)
  - optionally: staff position (for staff emergency/contact)

### **13.2 Data categories**

  - Contact category (contact\_person\_id methods)
  - Identity category (legal name, English name, DOB)
  - Address category
  - Medical/important notes category
  - Emergency policy access (break-glass)

### **13.3 Emergency “break-glass”**

Emergency data is sealed unless:

  - a privileged user declares emergency (policy + audit)
  - system logs access (who/when/why)

Some medical notes may need normal visibility (e.g., epilepsy) vs sealed emergency-only. We support per-field category.

## **14) Emergency policy (home-owned)**

Emergency policy belongs to a home and may include:

  - emergency contacts (multiple)
  - critical notes (allergies, meds, blood type if desired)
  - preferred hospital / instructions (optional)

School access:

  - only if granted by policy and/or break-glass.

## **15) Capability-based permissions (the big win)**

### **15.1 Goal**

Stop thinking “roles.” Think **capabilities**:

  - wildcard grants for whole spaces
  - explicit grants for specific tools
  - optional denies to carve out exceptions

### **15.2 Capability keys (hierarchical)**

Examples:

  - school.\*
  - school.classroom.\*
  - school.office.\*
  - school.dashboard.\*
  - school.office.calendar.view
  - school.office.calendar.edit
  - school.classroom.students.add
  - school.classroom.pin\_reset.initiate

### **15.3 Storage model**

We store capabilities per person per school:

  - school\_capability\_grants:
      
      - school\_id
      - person\_id
      - capability\_key (supports wildcards like school.office.\*)
      - effect = grant (default)
      - created\_by, timestamps
  - school\_capability\_denies:
      
      - same fields
      - effect = deny

**Evaluation rule**:

  - Most specific match wins
  - **Deny overrides grant**
  - Wildcards (\*) match prefixes

### **15.4 Hybrid UX (simple front end, clean backend)**

UI shows:

  - “Office access: Full ✅” (means grant school.office.\*)
  - Expand tools list with toggles
  - Turning one off adds a deny:
      
      - deny school.office.calendar.edit  
        No “exploding into 39 grants.”

### **15.5 Default bundles (not stored as a “role”)**

We can still use “role” as a UI preset:

  - Teacher preset → add grant school.classroom.\*
  - Admin preset → add grant school.office.\*
  - Director preset → add grant school.\*  
    But the truth is capabilities, not roles.

15.6 Routing + Student Modes (Desk vs Live Classroom)

  

Routing concepts:

  - Capabilities decide what SCHOOL spaces are visible/enterable (Office / Classroom / Dashboard).
  - Desk is NOT a school capability; Desk is the student’s personal workspace that can run in different contexts.

  

School container vs spaces:

  - school.\* routes to a School Container (a chooser/hub).
  - school.dashboard.\* routes to Dashboard.
  - school.office.\* routes to Office.
  - school.classroom.\* routes to Classroom.
  - The container only shows buttons for spaces the user can enter.

  

Student context rule (mode is NOT permission):

  - “Live Classroom” is entered only when:
      
      - a lesson exists for a section AND
      - the student is a participant in that lesson.
  - Otherwise the student is in Desk mode (personal or school-context).

  

Enrollment default:

  - An active enrollment implies the student has classroom access for that school

  (implemented as an implicit school.classroom.\* grant).

## **18) Open issues to keep visible (for next pass)**

#### **A. Home coordination, learners & consent**

  - Homes may include multiple adults (including unrelated adults / roommates)
  - A home may exist with **no homes.primary\_person\_id**
  - Any adult may **opt in** to become homes.primary\_person\_id (voluntary coordination role)
  - Homes.primary\_person\_id has **no automatic visibility or authority**
  - All visibility is **explicit, scoped, and revocable**

#### **B. Learner-specific nuance (important)**

**Adult learner case****  
** When a person becomes a learner (is\_learner = true) **without** having initialized as a student:

  - System prompts for visibility consent  
     Example:  
      
     “A Home Primary exists. Do you want to share your learning activity with them?”  
      
  - Default: **private unless explicitly shared**
  - Home Primary may request access  
     Learner may **accept / defer / decline**

Applies equally to:

  - Adult learning
  - Professional development
  - Therapy / support contexts

*NOTE:* Revisit a **“circle / consent graph”** abstraction if coordination becomes multi-directional or layered.

#### **C. Medical & sensitive information visibility**

  - Define information classes:
      
      - Normal access (consent-based)
      - Emergency-only (“break-glass” with audit trail)
  - Clarify:
      
      - Who may initiate emergency access
      - How access is logged

#### **D. School verification & claiming**

  - Decide between:
      
      - Minimal MVP claiming flow
      - Robust verification (proof, disputes, transfers)
  - Define when institutional authority becomes:
      
      - Asserted
      - Authoritative

#### **E. Scheduling model**

  - Instances computed vs stored
  - Substitutions, makeups, holidays
  - Truth (what happened) vs plan (what was scheduled)
