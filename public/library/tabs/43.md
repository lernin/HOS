1\. Overview

  

ENGA appears to be a periodic table of consciousness — fundamental elements of human experience. This report maps visible ENGA elements to Procedia's five‑category ontology.

2\. The Five Categories (Refresher)

  

Category Core Question Examples

Synergonym What is it like? What does it have? happy, on the table, can, should

Genonym What happens? eating, running, learning

Morphonym What pattern? schedule, routine, curve

Horonym What group/container? home, section, context

4\. Key Insight

  

The vast majority of ENGA elements are Synergonyms — they describe ways of relating to Autonyms. This makes sense: consciousness is not just about what exists, but about how we experience, evaluate, and relate to what exists.

  

Procedia Category Approx. % of ENGA

Autonym \~5%

Synergonym \~70%

Genonym \~15%

Morphonym \~5%

Horonym \~5%

  

\---

  

5\. What ENGA Adds That Procedia Didn't Have

  

Procedia's original ontology was strong on:

  

· Things (Autonyms)

· Parts/qualities/roles (Synergonyms)

· Events (Genonyms)

· Patterns (Morphonyms)

· Containers (Horonyms)

  

ENGA adds the phenomenological texture:

  

· Modality (can, could, should, must, might)

· Volition (want, desire)

· Affect (like, dislike)

· Social speech acts (offer, request, command)

· Temporal aspect (duration, completion, future)

· Negation patterns (deficiency, inability)
