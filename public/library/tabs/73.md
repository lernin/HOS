Alright Ashley — here’s the **surprisingly small set**. Humans mostly refer to entities using just a few patterns.

I’d break it into **6 main reference types**.

## **1️⃣ Proper Name**

The direct name of the entity.

Examples:

  - **Apple****  
      
    **
  - **Tim Cook****  
      
    **
  - **Seoul****  
      
    **
  - **Samsung****  
      
    **

These are the **strongest anchors**.

## **2️⃣ Pronoun**

Short grammatical references.

Examples:

  - he  
      
  - she  
      
  - it  
      
  - they  
      
  - his company  
      

Example:

Tim Cook spoke yesterday.  
 **He** announced a new product.

## **3️⃣ Role / Title**

Referring to a person or entity by **position or role**.

Examples:

  - the CEO  
      
  - the president  
      
  - the company  
      
  - the teacher  
      

Example:

Tim Cook introduced the device.  
 **The CEO** said demand is strong.

## **4️⃣ Descriptor**

A descriptive phrase.

Examples:

  - the tech giant  
      
  - the Cupertino company  
      
  - the smartphone maker  
      
  - the electric car pioneer  
      

Example:

Apple revealed a new chip.  
 **The tech giant** says it’s faster.

## **5️⃣ Relationship Reference**

Referencing via another entity.

Examples:

  - **Tim Cook’s company****  
      
    **
  - **the iPhone maker****  
      
    **
  - **Steve Jobs’ successor****  
      
    **

This requires **graph reasoning**.

## **6️⃣ Demonstrative Reference**

Pointing references.

Examples:

  - this company  
      
  - that firm  
      
  - those devices  
      

Example:

Apple released a new phone.  
 **This company** continues to dominate.

# **Why This Is Powerful**

Nearly every reference in text fits one of these:

name

pronoun

role

descriptor

relationship

demonstrative

So your system could do:

detect reference type

→ run appropriate resolver

Example:

pronoun → grammar resolver

descriptor → semantic matcher

relationship → knowledge graph
