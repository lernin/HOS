# Capacity  

### **A Note on Capacity**

Recently, the Korean government piloted digital textbooks in public schools. The intention was good, but the result was telling: parents reported that children were **exhausted, overstimulated, and overloaded**. Screens were always on. Interaction was constant. Cognitive demand never rested. The rollout was met with backlash—and ultimately pulled back.

This was not a failure of children.  
 It was a failure to respect **human learning limits**.

Children do not have unlimited time, energy, or emotional capacity. Their ability to learn fluctuates day to day, shaped by sleep, schedules, family life, peer relationships, classroom climate, and countless unseen factors. Any system that ignores this reality—even with the best intentions—will eventually overwhelm the learner.

Our approach starts from a different place:  
 **learning systems must adapt to children, not demand that children adapt to systems.**

Everything we build is designed to respect attention, conserve energy, and support learning only when the child is ready. Less noise. Fewer demands. Better timing. Sustainable growth.

This philosophy is not a reaction—it is the foundation.

  

## **Philosophical Foundation: Respecting Children’s Learning Capacity**

### **Core Belief**

Children do not have unlimited time, energy, or attention.  
Any learning system that assumes they do will eventually exhaust them.

Recent large-scale experiments with fully digital textbooks have shown this clearly: when digital systems demand constant engagement, children burn out—not because they are weak, but because they are human.

Our approach starts from a different premise:

**Learning capacity is limited, variable, and deeply contextual.**

### **Capacity Is Not Just “Time on Task”**

A child’s ability to learn at any given moment is shaped by many interacting factors, including:

  - physical and emotional energy
  - daily schedule load (school, hagwon, travel, sleep)
  - memory strength and cognitive style
  - motivation and attitude
  - home environment and family dynamics
  - peer relationships (supportive or hostile)
  - classroom climate and teacher quality

These factors **vary day to day**.  
A child who is capable and focused on Monday may be depleted on Tuesday—not because of ability, but because of life.

Any system that ignores this variability becomes punitive instead of supportive.

### **Our Design Principle**

The system adapts to the child — not the other way around.

Instead of pushing uniform workloads, fixed pacing, or constant stimulation, the AI:

  - observes engagement signals rather than forcing output
  - respects moments of low capacity
  - avoids unnecessary repetition or overload
  - gently stretches capacity when conditions are right
  - rewards honesty (“I don’t get this”) as much as confidence (“I know this”)

Learning is treated as a **dynamic process**, not a quota.

### **Less Is Often More**

We deliberately avoid “always-on” digital experiences.

  - Screens are quiet by default.
  - Review appears briefly, then disappears.
  - Opportunities are time-bound, not endless.

This teaches students that:

  - attention matters
  - engagement has value
  - rest is part of learning

The goal is **sustainable growth**, not short-term compliance.

### **The Role of AI**

AI is not used to pressure students.

It is used to:

  - notice patterns humans cannot easily track
  - respond to individual capacity profiles
  - personalize pacing without labeling or shaming
  - support teachers instead of replacing them

The system acts as a **gentle guide**, not a taskmaster.

### **Bottom Line**

Children are not empty containers.  
They are living systems with fluctuating capacity.

A learning platform that respects this reality:

  - reduces burnout
  - improves long-term outcomes
  - earns trust from students, parents, and teachers

This philosophy is not an add-on.  
It is the foundation of everything we build.
