# Knowledge Map  

White Paper 07

  

The Knowledge Map: A Taxonomic Framework for Human-Scale Learning

  

How to Organize, Measure, and Master All of Human Knowledge

  

Author: Ashley

Date: March 20, 2026

Series: Foundations of the atomonym System

  

\---

  

1\. Abstract

  

Human knowledge is vast, but it is not unstructured. This paper presents a complete framework for organizing all concepts into a single taxonomic tree rooted at entity, with depth levels representing conceptual specificity and frequency bands representing real-world word usage.

  

The framework enables:

  

· Visualization of the entire knowledge space

· Measurement of individual mastery at any depth and frequency

· Guided learning that respects human curiosity and cognitive limits

· Continuous expansion as new concepts are discovered

  

The result is a living map of knowledge that grows with its users.

  

\---

  

2\. The Tree Structure

  

2.1 Root and Branches

  

All concepts descend from a single root: entity.

  

\`\`\`

entity

├── physical\_object

├── abstract\_entity

├── event

├── place

├── time

├── relation

└── property

\`\`\`

  

Each node branches into increasingly specific Hyponyms, creating a pure taxonomic hierarchy.

  

2.2 Depth Levels

  

Concepts are assigned a depth level based on how many steps they are from entity:

  

Level Description Example

1 The root entity

2 Top categories physical\_object

3 Broad categories living\_thing

4 Basic types animal

5 Common kinds mammal

6 Specific kinds canine

7 Breeds / models dog

8 Sub-breeds poodle

9 Ultra-specific toy\_poodle

10+ Deeper specialization miniature\_toy\_poodle

  

The tree has no artificial cap — it extends as deep as reality demands.

  

\---

  

3\. Frequency Bands

  

Alongside depth, each concept is assigned a frequency band based on how common it is in everyday language.

  

3.1 The Five Bands

  

Band Frequency Range Description Example

1 Top 100 Ultra-common day, year

2 101-1,000 Very common hour, minute

3 1,001-5,000 Common century, moment

4 5,001-20,000 Uncommon decade, heartbeat

5 20,000+ Rare epoch, picosecond

  

3.2 Why Five Bands

  

Five bands provide enough granularity to be meaningful while remaining intuitive. Users can easily understand "Band 1" vs. "Band 5" without needing a fine-grained slider.

  

Bands are determined algorithmically from corpus data and can be adjusted when new evidence warrants reclassification.

This aligns perfectly with Zipf's law: a small number of very common words do most of the work, while the vast majority of words are rare and specialized.

6\. Learning Through the Map

  

6.1 Natural Learning Path

  

Humans learn in a predictable pattern:

  

Phase Depth Bands Activity

Tasting 3-4 1-2 Sampling everything — basic categories, common words

Exploring 5-6 1-3 Going deeper where curious, still broad

Passion 7-10 1-5 Diving deep into one area (dinosaurs, cars, space)

Mastery 10+ All Becoming an expert, learning rare and technical terms

7\. The Map Is Alive

  

The knowledge map is not static. It grows as:

  

· New concepts are discovered (picosecond added to duration)

· Existing concepts are reclassified (heartbeat moves from Band 4 to Band 3)

· Domains emerge (new technologies, new species, new ideas)

  

The tree has no artificial cap. If a concept justifies itself at level 20, it belongs at level 20. The map simply records reality.

9\. Conclusion

  

The Knowledge Map framework provides:

  

· A single rooted taxonomy for all human knowledge

· Depth levels representing conceptual specificity

· Frequency bands representing real-world word usage

· A mastery matrix for tracking individual progress

· A living structure that grows with discovery

  

It respects how humans actually learn — starting broad, going deep only where passion leads — while providing a rigorous underlying structure.

  

The map is not the territory. But it's the best guide we have.
